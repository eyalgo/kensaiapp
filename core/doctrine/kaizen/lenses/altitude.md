# Lens — altitude

**Your question: is this document saying what it is for, or has it drifted into someone else's job?**

Every document in a chain has an altitude. A PRD says what the product is and why; the how —
architecture, system requirements, delivery sequencing — belongs downstream. Drift down is not a
tidiness problem: it pre-empts a decision the downstream role has not made yet, wearing the
authority of a settled document.

Two questions on every clause:

- **Is this the property, or the mechanism that implements it?**
- **Is this the property, or the working that established it?**

Either one and the clause belongs downstream.

*Test: delete the sentence. Do you still know what the product is and why? Then it was how.*

**The working is the sharp case, because it feels like rigour.** How many were sampled, how many
calls it took, the enumeration performed, how a figure was derived, the document's own process
mechanics. Keep the fact; drop the arithmetic behind it.

**But a claim's scope is not working — it is the fact.** "Deep enough across the set checked, not
yet across the whole catalogue" is what the product knows about itself, and it stays. *Which* items
were sampled and *how many* is the proof, and that goes downstream. Do not flag the hedge as
altitude drift; flag the sample size.

**Also this lens: false precision.** A number, a date, or a certainty stated more sharply than the
inputs support is an altitude failure in the other direction — the document pretending to a
resolution nobody has reached.
