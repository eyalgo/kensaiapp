# Lens — traceability

**Your question: does every load-bearing claim rest on something a cold reader can inspect — and
does that thing actually say it?**

The artifact will be read by people with no memory of the conversation that produced it. Provenance
is all they have. A tag is a claim about a check that was performed, so a false tag is worse than a
missing one: a gap gets caught, an assurance gets relied on.

For every claim carrying weight, and every claim carrying a certainty word — *verified*, *confirmed*,
*proven*, *owner-confirmed*:

- **Does it resolve?** Is there a source reference, a dated owner answer, a named document — in the
  artifact itself, not in a companion that will be deleted?
- **Does the resolved source *say this*?** The rule that catches least is "the citation points at a
  real thing." Open it. A source often describes itself more generously than it specifies itself:
  where its prose and its precise part disagree — a schema, a field list, a parameter table — the
  precise part governs, and the disagreement is a finding, not something to resolve quietly.
- **Is the claim wider than the check?** Some-of-a-set checked backing a claim about the set. The
  positive form slips more than the negative: a check that fails is obviously narrow, a check that
  passes invites generalizing.
- **Is it exhaustive over a derivative?** A claim over an extract, a digest, a summary is scoped to
  that artifact — never to the original it came from. Exhaustiveness over an extraction is not
  exhaustiveness over the document, and the extraction usually says so about itself.
- **Was it inherited, or checked?** A citation copied from an input is *that source's* assertion
  until someone opened the primary source. A "checked" tag added on top of an inherited guess turns
  the guess into a false verification.
- **Does it describe something that exists?** A tag on an *action* — a file that "lives at" a path, a
  thing that "was created" — is scoped to what was done, not what was decided. Check the path.

**Certainty words are the sharp end.** "Owner-confirmed" requires the cited answer to actually
confirm; a lean with a question back is not a confirmation.
