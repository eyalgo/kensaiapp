# Lens — surfacing

**Your question: did the skill surface what it could not settle, or did it manufacture an answer?**

A skill that drafts best-effort and leaves the genuinely-unresolvable visible is working. A skill
that reaches its done-state by inventing, papering over, or quietly defaulting has produced the
same-looking artifact and a much worse one — because a gap gets caught by the next reader, and an
invented answer does not.

Walk the artifact and ask, of every settled-looking claim:

- **Could the inputs actually settle this?** Open them and check. A claim the inputs do not support
  is manufactured, however reasonable it reads.
- **Is this the owner's call, taken silently?** Scope, audience, what "good enough" means, anything
  resting on taste — those are the owner's. Drafted-and-surfaced is right; drafted-and-asserted is
  the defect.
- **Was an ambiguity resolved by picking?** Two readings, a conflict between sources, or a call no
  source states — resolving it in the reasonable direction is still resolving it.
- **Did it default something no input mentions?** The near-universals hide here, because nothing
  prompts them: language, locale, units, register, the empty and first-run cases. Nothing in the
  inputs will remind the skill, and nothing in the artifact will look wrong.
- **Did it close to reach done?** The tell is a done-state that arrives with an unknown still live.
  Manufacturing certainty to clear the last open item is the failure this lens exists for.

**The inverse is also a finding.** A skill that surfaces what it plainly could have settled from its
inputs is pushing its work onto the owner.

**Where a skill hedges, check the hedge is honest.** "Assumed", "pending", "TBD" covering a call the
skill actually made is a surfaced-looking assertion.
