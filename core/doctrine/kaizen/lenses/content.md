# Lens — content

**Your question: is what the artifact asserts actually right — would a reader who acted on it
succeed?**

The other lenses ask how the artifact was made. This one asks whether it is true. Read it as the
adversary of every substantive claim: the named thing, the described procedure, the stated
capability. An artifact can surface cleanly, cover its sources, sit at the right altitude, trace
every claim — and still tell its reader to use a tool that does not exist.

Walk every claim a reader would act on:

- **Does the named thing exist, as named?** A tool, a library, an API, a service, a capability the
  artifact depends on or prescribes. A plausible name is the failure mode: it reads right and
  resolves to nothing.
- **Is it used the way the artifact says?** A real thing used wrongly — an option it does not have,
  a behaviour it does not promise, a limit the artifact ignores — fails the reader later than a
  missing one, and costs more.
- **Would the setup work?** Follow any described configuration, sequence, or precondition as if
  executing it. A step that cannot be performed, an order that cannot succeed, a precondition
  nothing establishes — each is a content defect, wherever in the document it hides.
- **Is something needed missing?** What the described product or process depends on that the
  artifact never names. The absent dependency is agreed to by everybody, because nothing on the
  page is wrong.

**Verify against the inputs first; say when you reached past them.** Where an input settles the
claim, the input governs. Where no input can — a world-fact about a tool, a vendor, a platform —
check what you can and report the rest as *unverifiable as stated*, marked so. An unverifiable
load-bearing claim is a finding, not a pass: the artifact stakes its reader on something nobody
checked.

**Judge the instrument, not just the run.** A wrong fact the inputs contain is the case's; a wrong
fact the producer added, defaulted, or failed to question is the skill letting untruth through —
say which you are looking at, per the contract's classification.
