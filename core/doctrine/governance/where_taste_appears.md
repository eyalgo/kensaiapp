# Where taste appears — the aesthetic call is made in the run that owns it

Governs every document in a product's definition chain (`setup/setting_up_a_project.md` § The flow
at a glance) and every document a change is carried through (`flow/development_flow.md`).

> **Owns:** where an aesthetic call may be made, and what a document that does not own the call may
> say about it. **Does not own:** what the call is (the run that owns it), where a token value lives
> (`<CORE>/roles/loaders/cdo_global.md` § What you own — only the product's `DESIGN.md`), the order
> the documents are written in (`setup/setting_up_a_project.md`), or who owns a product's words (the
> Content Manager's `CONTENT.md`).

---

## The rule

**Taste never appears upstream.** The aesthetic call belongs to the run that owns it, is made there,
and is recorded with its reason. A document that does not own the call and carries a preference
anyway has made a decision that was not its to make, and has made it where nobody with the standing
to judge it will look.

**Upstream names every document other than the one the call belongs to** — a place in the chain of
ownership, not a position in time. The design language may settle after the SDD
(`setup/setting_up_a_project.md` § The flow at a glance), and the SDD is upstream of it all the same.

**The rule binds in both directions, which is why it is one rule and not two.** Upstream does not
decide taste; and the run that owns the call does not read an upstream document's silence on taste
as a gap to escalate. Silence there is correct — the call is already the run's.

## What a document upstream may say

The requirement, never the preference. A stated commitment is upstream's to make and can be checked
against; a mood word is the call itself, deferred by wording. *"Every destructive action is undoable
for 10s or confirmed"* belongs upstream; *"the product should feel forgiving"* does not, until it has
been translated into one (`<CORE>/../skills/cdo/.claude/skills/product-uxd/prompts/rich.md` § Tone).

## Both mediums, and the tell

The rule is the same wherever the call sits. The visual call is the design run's, recorded in the
product's `DESIGN.md`; the standard a product's words are written to is the Content Manager's,
recorded in `CONTENT.md` (`<CORE>/roles/loaders/cdo_global.md` § What you own). **The tell that the
seam has slipped is a run setting what it should be using** — a design run setting tone rather than
using it is the same defect as an upstream document choosing a colour.

## A lower layer may tighten this, never relax it

An instance or a project may **add** to this rule or **tighten** it: name a further document that may
carry no call, or narrow what counts as a requirement rather than a preference. It may **not** relax
it, remove it, or carve an exception out of it — an exception is how the call comes to be made
upstream after all, one document at a time. Where a lower layer's rule conflicts with this one,
**this one wins and the conflict is surfaced** rather than resolved in the lower layer's favour.
