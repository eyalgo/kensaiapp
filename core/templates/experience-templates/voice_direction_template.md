## 1. Overview

<How this instance's products should sound, who they are speaking to, and what a run authoring a
product's own words should fall back on when neither a limit nor an inherited line covers its case.
This is the section read exactly when nothing else answers, so it is held to the highest bar in the
document.

No product is described here, and no line from the sections below is restated here.>

## 2. Voice, tone and address

<Whether products speak as *we* or as the product; whether the reader is *you*; how formal, how
warm, and how that shifts when something has gone wrong. Each inherited line carries its marker —
what the markers are, and what an unmarked line means, are the prompt's § Markers.

Where the instance sets nothing here, one line saying so, marked `n/a`.>

## 3. Terminology

<Words this instance's products use, and words they do not — the shared vocabulary, and the
substitutions that go with it. A product's own feature names are not here; a rule about how names
are formed may be. Same marker treatment, or one `n/a` line.>

## 4. Message patterns

<What the recurring moments sound like across products: a failure, an empty state, something in
progress, something partly done, something recovered. An instance that holds one shape for these
saves every product from inventing it. Same marker treatment, or one `n/a` line.>

## 5. Reading bar and text accessibility

<The reading level products write to, and the requirements text carries for readers using assistive
technology — alternative text, link text that reads on its own, the treatment of abbreviations.
Requirements rather than aspirations, each pinned to the standard it holds. Same marker treatment,
or one `n/a` line.>

## 6. Localization

<Whether products are written to be translated, and what that requires of the source words — no
concatenated sentences, no meaning carried by word order, dates and numbers left to the locale.
Same marker treatment, or one `n/a` line.>
